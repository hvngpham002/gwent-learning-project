"Mastering Strategy Card Game (Legends of Code and Magic) via

## Annotation Batch

The annotation file now records:

- `pages_read: "1-10"`
- `status: "in-progress"`

The first batch covers:

- the paper's multi-stage strategy-card-game motivation;
- OSFP's smooth-best-response formulation and last-iterate convergence
  framing;
- DRL as a smooth-best-response sub-solver through policy gradients and
  opponent sampling;
- LoCM as a two-player zero-sum imperfect-information game;
- LoCM 1.2 and 1.5 card-building and battle-stage observation/action
  differences;
- the end-to-end policy architecture with shared card embeddings,
  card-building and battle action heads, and a single value head;
- legal-action masking in card-building and battle stages;
- V-Trace, UPGO, entropy regularization, and whole-game trajectory
  training;
- COG2022 headline results, internal matchup-matrix evaluation, and
  initial ablations;
- the start of the MCTS discussion, including the explicit ground-truth
  hidden-state expansion caveat.

## Risks And Follow-Ups

- `xi2023mastering` is not yet a completed annotation. Future specs may
  cite only the page-grounded factual notes already written, not a
  completed learning-roadmap decision.
- The local source has 17 pages. PDF pp. 11-17 still need a later
  explicit continuation batch before final decision sections can be
  completed.
- The reported system uses very large compute: the LoCM 1.5 model used
  24 V100/600-CPU-core Units for around 72 hours. This is not a
  near-term Gwent implementation mandate.
- LoCM is a CCG training benchmark, not a Gwent rules source. Its mana,
  HP, lane, creature, spell, generated-card, and construction mechanics
  must not be imported into Gwent gameplay.
- The page 10 MCTS experiment uses ground-truth hidden world state and
  must not be treated as a hidden-safe product AI observation boundary.
