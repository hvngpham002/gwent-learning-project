## Behavior Changed

cFp81 adds a new benchmark-only artifact family:

```text
search-consumer-action-feature-casebook-v0/cFp81
```

It reads committed cFp80 artifacts, validates cFp80 readiness, validates row
counts and dictionary references, expands dictionary ids only in memory for
grouping, writes bounded aggregate casebook rows and slice summaries, and
carries inherited skipped-root counts beside the resulting summaries.

cFp81 does not:

- modify cFp80, cFp79, cFp78, or cFp76 artifact directories;
- execute candidate actions;
- rebuild sampled states;
- materialize hidden worlds;
- re-observe benchmark roots;
- run rollout search, PIMC evaluation, or ISMCTS/MCTS;
- compute action values, expected values, reward targets, win probabilities,
  payoff tables, or principal variations;
- rank actions or choose best actions;
- change gameplay behavior, engine rules, legal moves, AI policy behavior,
  sampler behavior, deck/catalog data, benchmark suite definitions, or rating
  artifacts;
- expose AI Lab runner buttons or wire product search AI.

The only product-surface change is static AI Lab metadata pointing at cFp81 as
casebook/readiness infrastructure.

## Risks And Follow-Ups

- cFp81 describes coverage and consumer-readiness slices but does not analyze
  action quality.
- cFp82 should be a decision phase that selects the next benchmark-only
  consumer step from the casebook evidence.
- cFp82 must still avoid rollout, value estimation, action ranking,
  best-action selection, win probability, reward targets, search gameplay, and
  product AI.

## Recommended Next Step

cFp82 should be a benchmark-only decision phase that uses the cFp81 casebook to
choose between a feature-consumer contract, a deterministic subset for small
consumer experiments, or a pause for review. It must remain non-gameplay and
must not add rollout, value, ranking, best-action, win-probability,
reward-target, search gameplay, or product AI.
