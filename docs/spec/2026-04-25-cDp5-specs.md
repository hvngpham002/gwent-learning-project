# Implementation Spec: Cluster D, Phase 5 - Engine AI Policy V0

## Goal

Replace the opt-in engine shell's pass-only scripted preview AI with a bounded engine AI policy that selects from engine legal moves. The first AI policy should be deterministic, simple, seat-neutral, and safe. It should prove the architecture: AI receives legal moves, chooses one legal move, dispatches the exact corresponding engine command, and never uses the legacy AI/rule path.

This is not full strategic AI parity. It is the first migration slice from "AI always passes" to "AI can play legal engine actions."

## Architecture Position

Current state after `cDp4`:

- legacy UI remains the default route;
- engine UI is opt-in through `?engine=1` or `VITE_ENGINE_UI=1`;
- the engine shell starts current Northern Realms vs Nilfgaard catalog presets;
- human actions use engine legal moves for mulligan, card play, leader use, prompt choice, pass, and round resolution;
- AI currently uses `scriptedPreviewAi` behavior:
  - zero-card mulligan;
  - pass on its playing turn;
  - no card play;
  - no leader use;
  - no non-trivial prompt resolution.

Phase 5 should replace that preview behavior with a small legal-move policy. The engine remains the rule authority. React remains an adapter.

## Scope

### 1. Add an engine AI policy module

Create a new engine AI policy path. Suggested location:

- `src/game/ai/`

Suggested files:

- `src/game/ai/types.ts`
- `src/game/ai/seatObservation.ts`
- `src/game/ai/legalMoveCommands.ts`
- `src/game/ai/legalHeuristicPolicyV0.ts`
- `src/game/ai/index.ts`

The exact file split can differ, but keep the policy outside React and outside Redux.

Required concepts:

```ts
interface EnginePolicy {
  id: string;
  selectMove(input: EnginePolicyInput): LegalMove | null;
}

interface EnginePolicyInput {
  seatId: SeatId;
  observation: SeatObservation;
  legalMoves: readonly LegalMove[];
}
```

The policy selects a legal move, not a hand-written command.

### 2. Add a seat observation builder

Add a small observation model for policy input.

Minimum `SeatObservation` data:

- acting seat id;
- opponent seat id;
- phase;
- round number;
- current turn;
- own hand card summaries;
- own leader status;
- own deck/discard counts;
- own passed/gem state;
- opponent hand count only;
- opponent leader public status;
- opponent deck/discard counts;
- opponent passed/gem state;
- public board summaries by seat and row;
- public weather entries;
- current score totals and row totals from the shared scoring pipeline;
- pending prompt summary when visible to the acting seat.

Hidden-info rule:

- Default AI observation may include own hand details.
- Default AI observation must not include opponent hand card IDs, source IDs, names, rows, abilities, or deck order.
- A future debug/cheat observation may be added later, but do not add it in this phase unless needed for tests and clearly named as debug-only.

### 3. Add legal move to command conversion

Add a helper that converts a selected legal move into the exact engine command.

Suggested function:

```ts
function commandFromLegalMove(move: LegalMove): Exclude<EngineCommand, { type: "StartMatch" }> | null
```

It must support:

- `choose_mulligan` -> `ChooseMulligan`
- `play_card` -> `PlayCard`
- `pass` -> `Pass`
- `use_leader` -> `UseLeader`
- `choose_prompt_option` -> `ChoosePromptOption`
- optionally `resolve_round_end` -> `ResolveRoundEnd`

Use exact fields from the legal move:

- exact `seatId`;
- exact `sourceCardId`;
- exact `target`;
- exact `promptId`;
- exact `optionId`;
- exact `cardIds`.

Do not reconstruct targets from labels or metadata.

### 4. Implement `LegalHeuristicPolicyV0`

Implement one deterministic policy.

Policy id:

- `legal-heuristic-v0`

Required behavior:

1. If legal moves are empty, return `null`.
2. During mulligan, choose the legal zero-card mulligan move if available.
   - Smart mulligan is out of scope.
3. During prompt resolution, choose one legal `choose_prompt_option` move deterministically.
   - Prefer the highest visible target strength if the observation has enough data.
   - Otherwise tie-break by move id.
4. During playing, choose among legal `play_card`, implemented `use_leader`, and `pass` moves.
5. Use deterministic scoring and tie-breaks only.
   - No `Math.random()`.
   - No timers.
   - No date/time input.
6. Prefer legal card plays that improve the AI's public board/score position.
7. Prefer `Pass` when:
   - no useful non-pass move exists; or
   - the opponent has already passed and the AI is ahead.
8. Do not select a placeholder leader move.
   - `cDp4` should already prevent placeholder leader moves from being legal; keep the policy defensive anyway.
9. Return a legal move object from the input list.

Simple ranking is acceptable. Suggested V0 ranking:

- prompt choice;
- zero-card mulligan;
- if opponent passed and AI is ahead, pass;
- beneficial `play_card` moves ranked by:
  - unit/hero printed strength from own hand summary;
  - useful row/horn/weather/public board context when easily available;
  - source and target metadata;
- useful `use_leader` only when public weather exists for Clear Weather;
- pass;
- deterministic `moveId` tie-break.

This policy may be mediocre. Correct legal behavior matters more than strategic quality.

### 6. Wire policy into the engine shell

Replace the pass-only scripted preview AI in `EngineGameManager.tsx` with the new policy adapter.

Required shell behavior:

- AI still completes mulligan after human mulligan is complete.
- When it is the AI seat's playing turn and no blocking human prompt exists, AI chooses a legal policy move.
- If AI has a pending prompt, AI may choose a legal prompt option.
- Human prompts must remain human-controlled.
- The policy dispatches through `dispatchEngineCommand`.
- The UI must not locally mutate board, hand, weather, prompt, score, pass, or round state after AI acts.
- The engine shell should still avoid timers for rule transitions.

It is acceptable for AI actions to happen immediately after render. Presentation delays can come later.

### 7. Keep round resolution explicit

For this phase, do not auto-resolve `round_end` in the UI unless the implementation can do so without introducing timing/race risk and tests it clearly.

Preferred behavior:

- AI can make mulligan, prompt, playing, card, leader, and pass decisions.
- Human or existing UI control still resolves `round_end`.

The policy helper may support `resolve_round_end` conversion for future AI-vs-AI/simulation use, but wiring automatic round resolution is optional and should be documented.

### 8. Preserve hidden-info discipline in UI

The engine shell must still show AI hand count only by default.

Do not render:

- AI hand card names;
- AI hand source IDs;
- AI hand images;
- AI deck order;
- debug observation details that expose hidden AI or human information.

Policy tests may inspect observation objects directly.

### 10. Add focused tests

Add or update tests for policy and shell adapter behavior.

Suggested tests:

- `tests/game/engineAiPolicy.test.ts`
- update or replace `tests/components/scriptedPreviewAi.test.ts`
- extend `tests/store/engineAdapter.test.ts` only if helpful.

Minimum coverage:

1. Observation builder includes own hand summaries but only opponent hand count.
2. `commandFromLegalMove` preserves exact command payloads for every supported legal move kind.
3. Policy returns `null` when no legal moves exist.
4. Policy chooses zero-card mulligan when available.
5. Policy chooses a legal `play_card` move rather than pass when playable moves exist and opponent has not already passed ahead.
6. Policy can choose `pass` when opponent has passed and AI is ahead.
7. Policy chooses a legal `choose_prompt_option` move for an AI-owned prompt.
8. Policy is seat-neutral and works for `seat_a` and `seat_b`.
9. Policy is deterministic for the same input.
10. Engine shell or controller helper does not dispatch when:
    - no match exists;
    - adapter lock blocks action;
    - game is over;
    - prompt belongs to the human seat;
    - current turn belongs to human during playing.
11. A Redux/engine flow can start a match, complete mulligans, let AI choose and dispatch at least one legal card play, and update engine board/hand/event state.
12. AI never dispatches a command that is not derived from a legal move.
13. Forbidden import check includes the new engine AI policy path.

## Notes For The Coding Instance

- Keep V0 boring. A legal, deterministic, weak policy is better than a clever policy that rebuilds rules.
- The policy should choose legal move objects first, then convert to commands. Do not hand-write a command and hope it is legal.
- Be careful with prompt locks. `dispatchEngineCommand` can process prompt commands when the lock belongs to the prompted seat.
- Do not solve every strategy edge case. Make the first AI card play through the engine path and leave heuristic quality for later.
- If the policy needs metadata that legal moves do not expose, prefer adding observation fields derived from public state or own-hand catalog data. Do not add hidden opponent-hand data to policy input.
